AI sounds confident. It always sounds confident. That's the problem.

Wrong answers come out the same way right answers do — fluent, structured, authoritative. There's no hesitation, no "I'm not sure about this." Just a paragraph that sounds completely correct and might not be.

AI generates text by predicting what words should come next based on patterns in its training data. That process doesn't know the difference between true and plausible. Both come out sounding the same.

## Where AI gets it wrong most often

| Type | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Recent info** | Doesn't know what changed after its training cutoff | New laws, software versions |
| **Numbers & stats** | Invents plausible-sounding figures | "Studies show 73% of..." |
| **Citations** | Cites papers and books that don't exist | "According to Smith (2019)..." |
| **Proper nouns** | Gets names, dates, roles wrong | Person's career history, event dates |
| **Code** | Syntactically fine but broken or insecure | Using APIs that don't exist |
| **Expert domains** | Subtle errors in medicine, law, tax, finance | Wrong dosage, wrong legal interpretation |

## The rules

**Verify important information at the source**
Use AI to understand and explore, but for anything that matters — check the official docs, the original paper, the primary source. Don't skip this step.

```
✅ AI answer → use to understand → verify at primary source → decide
❌ AI answer → act on it directly
```

**Ask for sources — then check those too**
Asking AI "can you give me a source for that?" is useful. But AI-provided citations are themselves unreliable. The paper might not exist, or say something different. Look it up.

```
Can you give me a source or official documentation link for this?
```

**Push back when something feels off**
If an answer seems wrong or weird, say so. Don't just move on.

```
Can you double-check that answer?
Are there any exceptions or counterarguments to this?
```

**Get professional review for high-stakes domains**
Medical, legal, financial, tax information — use AI to get oriented, never as the final word. A professional reviewing your AI-assisted work is not optional in these areas.

## Real examples of what goes wrong

**Made-up citations**
Ask AI for research on a topic → AI produces a paper title, author name, and year that all sound real → search for it and it doesn't exist.

**Outdated code**
Ask how to use a library → AI answers based on an old version → the function doesn't exist in the current version or behaves differently.

**Wrong legal interpretation**
Ask AI for help with a contract clause → AI answers confidently → actual legal interpretation is different from what was described.

## The bottom line

> **Trust AI, but verify.**

Treat every AI response as a first draft from a knowledgeable but fallible collaborator. The fluency is real. The accuracy is not guaranteed.
